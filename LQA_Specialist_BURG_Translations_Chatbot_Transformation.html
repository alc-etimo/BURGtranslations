A significant challenge I faced — and how I overcame it

After leading the migration and hygiene of 15,000 knowledge-base assets into a single CRM, I designed and led a company-wide redesign of chatbot content architecture so that long, multi-step regulatory and technical procedures became scannable decision paths that call-center agents could actually use within a ~4-minute AHT.

Role: Lead Linguist Scope: 15K assets · 30K+ agents Domain: Telecom · Regulatory · Financial

Case Study

AI Chatbot Content Optimization — turning dense knowledge-base tables into guided, decision-tree flows that operators could follow under real-time pressure.

AI Chatbot Content Optimization

Challenge

After unifying 15K assets, regulatory and technical content remained long multi-step blocks in simple HTML tables. Checklist and Action lived side-by-side; Yes/No options shared the same cell. Agents with ~4 min AHT could not scan or follow the paths, so the content was barely used despite the migration effort.

Solution

I designed a vertical architecture within the limited HTML editor: question in a merged header row, Yes and No each in their own column with anchors to specific instructions. Validated with an IBM engineer and stakeholders, it became a company-wide project to convert the full set of chatbot contents. I led the work, starting with high-impact financial flows.

Results

Architecture validated by IBM engineer and stakeholders, then rolled out company-wide. We started with financial flows (highest business impact). Within one week, one of the redesigned flows ranked among the top 2% most accessed assets out of 15,000. Adoption by agents was immediate; the same pattern was applied across remaining high-volume topics.

How I approached the obstacle

I had already led the linguistic side of the migration: unifying content from multiple platforms, removing duplicates, and consolidating equivalent articles so the knowledge base was clean. Even after that hygiene, the remaining content — especially regulatory, legal and technical — still failed in production. Weekly access reports showed almost no usage. The reason was structural: procedures lived in simple two-column HTML tables (Checklist | Action). Yes and No branches shared the same cell, making paths invisible and unscannable for operators under a ~4-minute AHT.

Working inside the constraints of the HTML editor, I proposed a vertical layout: the question occupied a merged header row; Yes and No each received their own column, with anchors that jumped to the corresponding instruction block. The design was reviewed with an IBM engineer and with business stakeholders, then approved as a company-wide initiative.

As lead, I received process flowcharts from other areas, translated them into chatbot decision trees (respecting a hard limit of five response buttons), and sometimes corrected process logic that did not fit the channel. I produced language and adaptation guidelines, button-copy rules, and A/B tests with live operators. We prioritised financial topics for maximum business impact. Flows were authored in Excel, compiled via SQL, and published into the CRM. Adoption followed immediately: one of the first converted flows entered the top 2% of all 15K assets within a week.
