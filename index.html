03.1 / SEVERITY CALIBRATIONPROJECT-SPECIFIC

This visual scale adapts the Critical / Major / Minor / Not Present distinction from an independent syntax-audit portfolio to a professional LQA reporting example. Severity depends on impact and the approved project specification—not on personal preference.

CRITICAL

Meaning is reversed, a key instruction is omitted, or the target could cause a material misunderstanding or high-risk action.

Disposition: correct and escalate according to the project rules.

MAJOR

A substantial accuracy, completeness, or required-terminology issue changes interpretation or use.

Disposition: correct before delivery; check for recurrence.

MINOR

A localized grammar, punctuation, or style issue remains understandable and does not materially change meaning.

Disposition: correct according to project acceptance criteria.

NO ERROR / ACCEPTABLE

A valid locale variant or stylistic choice that does not violate the brief, glossary, or style guide.

Disposition: retain; avoid false-positive corrections.

From finding to action

  1. Correct: fix meaning-changing errors before delivery.
  2. Investigate: look for repeated patterns in polarity, terminology, or locale usage.
  3. Prevent: update the checklist, style guidance, or termbase where justified.
  4. Verify: sample related segments after the change and record disposition.
